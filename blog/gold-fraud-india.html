Over 1 lakh people lost ₹1,404 crore invested in a gold company, Tamil Nadu Police records show. Most gold frauds are planned before you pay.

The company was Aarudhra Gold Trading. Most gold frauds work like this: someone wins your trust first, then takes your gold or money. Here are the six common ways, with real cases and the check that stops each one.

Tamil Nadu Police records, as of June 2023, reported in November 2025. Source: Free Press Journal, a daily newspaper.

By Mayank Gola, TrendTurtles  ·  10 min read  ·  Published: October 2026

Aarudhra, based in Tamil Nadu, allegedly promised people monthly returns of 10% to 30%. By June 2023, 1,04,433 people had asked it for about ₹2,438 crore back. Police records show that ₹1,404 crore of that was never paid back, the Free Press Journal reported in November 2025.

The police unit for big money frauds, the Economic Offences Wing, has filed charges against 40 people and firms.

Aarudhra is one example of a vanishing gold scheme. It is one of six common ways gold buyers are cheated.

Why Gold Is Easy to Cheat With

You cannot see purity by looking. Only a test shows how much gold a piece really has, as the first case below shows.

Most buyers cannot read the stamp. A real hallmark carries a six-character code that anyone can check. In an April 2025 survey by LocalCircles, a citizen survey platform, only 18% of 29,853 buyers said their jewellery had a code of that length.

The extra charges have no fixed rate. Two researchers at IIM Kozhikode wrote in 2024 that making charges are "highly variable and is opaque to the consumer". In simple words: you cannot see how the shop sets them.

Many buyers keep no paper. In a 2024 LocalCircles survey, 8% of gold jewellery buyers said they took no GST bill, because they paid in cash.

Pledged gold sits where you cannot see it. When you pledge gold for a loan, it goes into a lender's vault. You hold only a receipt until you repay.

Gold Fraud: The Six Common Ways, With Real Cases

1. Fake hallmarks

The case. In October 2026, a woman came to a jewellery shop in Kanpur to sell a gold chain and two rings. The chain carried an 18-carat hallmark, which means 75% gold. The shop's test found only about 10% gold. Police traced the jewellery to a shop in the city and seized a hallmarking machine there, Amar Ujala reported.

How it works. A machine stamps the purity mark on gold that is far less pure. Sometimes the fake mark comes from a hallmarking centre itself. In August 2026, BIS, the government body that runs hallmarking, raided a centre in Kadapa and seized a laser machine allegedly used to print fake BIS hallmarks.

What protects you. The six-character code on a real hallmark can be checked free in the BIS Care app. If hallmarked gold is less pure than its mark, BIS rules say you are owed two times the value of the missing gold. Read the full episode: fake gold hallmarks, and the free check that catches them.

2. Fake trading apps

The case. In 2025, a gems trader in Jaipur got a WhatsApp link from someone calling herself Kritika Thakur. The link opened a website that claimed high returns on gold trading. He put in ₹5 lakh, then about ₹6 crore in instalments. When he tried to take money out, he was told that 30% would be cut as "income tax". Over several payments, he lost ₹12 crore in all, ETV Bharat reported.

How it works. A stranger sends a link on WhatsApp or social media. The site shows your money growing. When you try to withdraw, it asks for a "tax" or a fee first. In Mangaluru in 2025, a man says an app found through Instagram showed his money at ₹60 lakh, then asked for 30% as "tax" before he could take it out. He says he lost ₹10.55 lakh.

What protects you. Never pay a "tax" or fee to take out your own money.

3. Vanishing gold schemes

The case. Investors in Heera Gold Exim were promised a 36% dividend, a share of profit, on the money they put in. When the company stopped paying, investors in several states complained of cheating. In October 2024, the Supreme Court cancelled the bail of the company's managing director and told her to go back to jail. She had not raised ₹580 crore to pay investors' claims. During the hearing, a judge asked: "Where is the money?"

How it works. The scheme promises very high returns. Early payouts and rewards bring in more people. Aarudhra, for example, allegedly offered gold coins, monthly payouts and a 2% commission for bringing in others. Then the payments stop.

What protects you. Treat a very high promised return as the warning. For a jeweller's own savings scheme, a legal explainer on LiveLaw says one that runs longer than a year could be treated as an unregulated deposit, which the law bans. So join only one that ends within a year, and keep a receipt for every payment.

4. Pledged gold taken or swapped

The case. To pledge gold is to hand it over as security for a loan. In 2010, 494 farmers in Kallakurichi district, Tamil Nadu, pledged their gold with cooperative societies, the local bodies that lend to farmers. Together they pledged about 1,800 sovereigns; a sovereign is 8 grams. Many pledged it for their children's weddings, or to cover losses from crop damage. When they went to take it back, they were told it had been stolen, a farmers' union leader said. In January 2026, hundreds of farmers protested in Chennai. They said gold and money were missing from at least 75 cooperative societies, The News Minute reported. These are the farmers' allegations.

How it works. Someone with access to the vault takes the gold, swaps it for fakes, or pledges it again somewhere else. In Thoothukudi, Tamil Nadu, police say members' gold was taken from a cooperative society. It was then pledged again at other banks, in the names of more than 10 people, including a car driver and domestic workers.

Inside banks, the appraiser is the person who checks and values your gold. In 2026, police arrested the appraiser at a Bank of Baroda branch in Eluru, Andhra Pradesh. He is accused of taking pledged gold from 176 gold loan accounts, worth about ₹10 crore. In Kakinada, a former appraiser of the same bank is accused of replacing about 1,057 grams of pledged gold, worth about ₹1.45 crore, with artificial ornaments.

What protects you. For loans from banks and finance companies, the RBI's 2025 rules for gold loans say the lender must return your gold within seven working days after you repay, and must check it with you against the loan papers. If the delay is the lender's fault, it must pay you ₹5,000 for each day late. If your gold is lost or found less pure, it must compensate you. These rules cover banks and finance companies, not farmers' cooperative societies.

5. Gold left with the jeweller

The case. Mansingh Gurjar's son was getting married in February. Gurjar had 640 grams of gold jewellery made by a jeweller in Gwalior. In the wedding rush, the jeweller gave it without a hallmark and said he would get it hallmarked later. After the wedding, Gurjar took the jewellery back to him. The jeweller said his system was down, kept the jewellery and gave him a receipt. Two days later, the shop was shut and the jeweller was gone, NPG News reported in June 2026. Once Gurjar went to the police, dozens of others came forward. The jeweller and his brother are accused of taking more than 3.5 kg of gold, worth about ₹4 crore, from over 20 customers. Police arrested them on the Meerut highway.

How it works. The jeweller holds your gold for a reason that sounds normal: remaking, repair or hallmarking. Then the jeweller denies it, or disappears.

What protects you. Do not take unhallmarked pieces on a promise to hallmark them later. When you leave gold, get a receipt that shows its weight. A receipt cannot stop a jeweller who runs away, but it is your proof. In Kangra, Himachal Pradesh, a jeweller denied receiving 32 grams of gold given for remaking in 2023. The customer showed the receipt, and a consumer court ordered the jeweller to return the gold and pay ₹35,000.

6. Making charge and wastage billed higher than promised

The case. A government teacher in Bangalore was promised 3–5% wastage in a jeweller's gold scheme. Wastage is the gold a shop says is lost while making a piece. She was charged 23.5%. She paid ₹78,300 for a necklace that should have cost ₹71,000, and in 2018 a consumer court ordered the jeweller to pay her back, Indian Jeweller reported.

How it works. A low rate is said aloud, and a higher one appears on the bill. Or the wastage is hidden inside the gold rate, so you cannot see it. In 2018, Legal Metrology officials, who check weights and prices in shops, raided jewellery showrooms in Hyderabad. The Hans India reported that in some cases about 25% extra was being charged as wastage. The rule they enforced is that wastage must be shown on the bill on its own.

What protects you. A bill that shows the wastage on its own line, as the rule says. Read the full episode: gold making charges and wastage, and how jewellers overcharge.

What It Costs Families

It takes what families saved for. The Tamil Nadu farmers' union says many farmers have died by suicide over the missing gold.

It is not rare. Between January 2023 and April 2025, BIS checked 8,580 jewellery shops in Tamil Nadu. It seized about 21.21 kg of jewellery, worth ₹19.27 crore, with fake hallmarks or no proper hallmark. In Kashmir in 2025, the head of BIS there said about fake hallmarks: "around 10 per cent of jewellers are involved". In simple words: about one jeweller in ten.

It is often found only later. In 2024, LocalCircles wrote that many buyers learn their gold is less pure than they paid for when they try to remake or sell it.

What To Do Before You Pay, Pledge or Invest

  1. Check the hallmark code in the free BIS Care app before you pay. If you doubt the gold later, a BIS-recognised centre tests it for ₹200.
  2. Never pay a "tax" or fee to take out your own money. Stop, and tell the police.
  3. Walk away from very high promised returns. Join a jeweller's scheme only if it ends within a year, and keep every receipt.
  4. When you repay a gold loan, check the gold before you leave. If a bank or finance company is late returning it, the RBI rules require it to pay you ₹5,000 a day.
  5. Get a receipt with the weight whenever you leave gold with a jeweller.
  6. Get the making charge and wastage written on the bill before you pay.
  7. If it goes wrong, complain at once. Call the National Consumer Helpline on 1915. For a fraud, go to the police. Against a jeweller or a lender, you can also go to a consumer court.

Who needs this most:

  • A family buying for a wedding: steps 1, 5 and 6.
  • Someone saving in a scheme or an app: steps 2 and 3.
  • A farmer or family pledging gold for a loan: step 4.
  • Anyone who has already lost gold: step 7.

Key Takeaways

  • Gold is easy to cheat with because you cannot see purity, the charges have no fixed rate, and many buyers keep no paper.
  • The six common tricks are fake hallmarks, fake trading apps, vanishing gold schemes, pledged gold taken or swapped, gold left with the jeweller, and making charges billed higher than promised.
  • Each trick has a warning sign you can learn, and a check or a right that protects you.
  • Keep paper for everything: the bill, the receipt and the loan papers.
