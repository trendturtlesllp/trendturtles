Each AMC's gold ETF moves differently. Gold fell −1.9%, Aditya Birla Sun Life's gold ETF −9.7% and Choice's −3.6%.

This was on 22 January 2026, the day after a rush to buy gold ETFs.

By Mayank Gola, TrendTurtles  ·  6 min read  ·  Published: November 2026

Which gold ETF is best? A gold ETF, or exchange traded fund, is a fund that buys gold with people's money. Many companies that run funds, called AMCs, offer one. Since they all buy gold, you might expect their prices to move together. On 22 January 2026, they did not.

This article follows three of them: Aditya Birla Sun Life's and Choice's, which fell the most and the least that day, and Nippon India's, the one people bought and sold the most that day. It starts with what each one holds.

1. Which Gold ETF Is Best? Start With What Each One Holds

Nippon India's gold ETF says it follows the price of gold "through physical gold". Aditya Birla Sun Life's says it "will invest in physical gold". Choice's says it invests "in physical gold and gold-related instruments". In simple words: all three buy gold.

Each fund is sold in small pieces called units, and each unit has two numbers. The first is its NAV, the value of the gold behind it. The NAV goes up and down with the price of gold. The second is its price on the stock market, the price you pay on your app. Buyers and sellers decide this price, so it can move away from the NAV.

So the NAV of all three should move with gold. Did their prices move with gold too?

So the same fall in gold became very different falls in price. Why did some prices fall so much more than the gold?

3. Why Some Prices Fell More Than the Gold

The answer is in the day before. On 21 January, people bought and sold ₹3,299.53 crore of Nippon India's gold ETF alone. Five days earlier, on 16 January, it was ₹767.82 crore. When so many people want to buy at once, prices can go above the NAV. Our article on ETF prices explains why. At the end of 21 January, the three were this far above their NAV:

By the end of 22 January, that extra was almost gone. Aditya Birla Sun Life's price was only 0.7% above its NAV. So its buyers saw the fall in gold, and they also lost most of the 9.4% extra they had paid. Nippon India's buyers had paid 6.3% extra, and their price fell less. Choice's buyers had paid only 2.6% extra, and by the end of 22 January its price was 0.9% above its NAV, so its price fell the least of the three.

But 21 and 22 January were not normal days. On a normal day, do gold ETF prices still move apart?

4. On a Normal Day, the Big Difference Is Trading

Take a recent day, 6 November 2026. That day, every gold ETF with a NAV in AMFI's list ended within 0.5% of it. Aditya Birla Sun Life's was 0.37% above, Nippon India's 0.19% and Choice's 0.15%. So on a normal day, all three were priced close to the gold inside them.

What differed was how much of each one people bought and sold. That day it was ₹538.93 crore for Nippon India's, ₹3.68 crore for Aditya Birla Sun Life's and ₹0.13 crore for Choice's.

This matters when you buy or sell. When few people trade an ETF, the price you can buy at and the price you can sell at can be far apart. Business Standard wrote in 2021 that in such an ETF, "A considerable portion of the returns earned from such an ETF will get wiped out by the large bid-ask spread". In simple words: the gap between the buying and selling price can eat up your gain.

There is one more difference, which you pay every year whether prices move or not. How much does each fund take for its costs?

5. The Yearly Cost

Every fund takes a small share of its money, a little each day, to pay its costs. This is called the expense ratio. On 31 October 2026, Nippon India's gold ETF took 0.81% a year, Choice's 0.51% and Aditya Birla Sun Life's 0.44%. Each includes GST, a tax.

Aditya Birla Sun Life's ETF costs the least, but on 21 January its buyers paid the biggest extra. Which gold ETF is best depends on what you check, and when you buy. How do you compare them before you buy?

6. How to Compare Gold ETFs Before You Buy

  1. Check the extra. During the day, look at the iNAV, an estimate of the NAV. If the price on your app is far above it, you pay extra. Our ETF article shows where to find the iNAV.
  2. Check how much it trades. On your app or the NSE website, look at how much of the ETF was bought and sold that day.
  3. Check the yearly cost. Find the expense ratio on the fund's website or AMFI's cost list.

If you already hold a gold ETF: do step 3 for the one you hold, and step 1 before you sell or add more. We do not tell anyone to buy, keep or sell. An adviser registered with SEBI can look at what you own.

If you are buying your first gold ETF: do all three steps, and compare at least two funds.

If you put in money every month: do step 1 each time you buy, because the extra can change.

Key Takeaways

  • The NAV of a gold ETF follows gold; its price on the stock market may not.
  • A rush to buy can push some gold ETFs far above their NAV.
  • On 6 November, prices stayed near the NAV, but low trading can cost you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do gold ETFs hold real gold?

All three in this article say they invest in physical gold (see section 1).

Why is a gold ETF's price different from its NAV?

Buyers and sellers decide the price, but the NAV comes from the gold the fund owns (see section 1).

Is a gold ETF better than digital gold?

Gold ETF units have no GST, while digital gold costs 3% GST. Our article on gold ETFs and digital gold compares them in full.
