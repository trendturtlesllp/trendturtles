Which gold ETF is best? A gold ETF, or exchange traded fund, is a fund that buys gold with people's money. Many companies that run funds, called AMCs, offer one.

This article follows three gold ETFs: Aditya Birla Sun Life's and Choice's, which moved the most and the least on 22 January 2026, and Nippon India's, which traded the most that day. First, what does each one hold?

1. Which Gold ETF Is Best? Start With What Each One Holds

Nippon India's gold ETF says it follows the price of gold "through physical gold". Aditya Birla Sun Life's says it "will invest in physical gold". Choice's says it invests "in physical gold and gold-related instruments". In simple words: all three buy gold.

Each fund is sold in small pieces called units. Each unit's NAV is the value of the gold behind it, so the NAV moves with the price of gold. Each unit also has a price on the stock market, and buyers and sellers decide that price. So the price can move differently from the gold.

For a few funds, such as LIC MF, Tata and Invesco India, the NAV fell by a different amount. The NSE and AMFI files do not say why. Why did the prices fall so much more than the NAV?

3. Why: The Extra Paid the Day Before

On 21 January, people bought and sold ₹3,299.53 crore of Nippon India's gold ETF alone. On 16 January, it was ₹767.82 crore. When many people want to buy, the price can go above the NAV. Our article on ETF prices explains why. At the end of that day, these three ETFs were this far above their NAV:

The next day, most of that extra went away. What about a normal day?

4. On a Normal Day: Price and Trading

On 6 November 2026, the price of every gold ETF that had a NAV that day ended within 0.5% of it. Aditya Birla Sun Life's was 0.37% above, Nippon India's 0.19% and Choice's 0.15%.

What was very different was how much each one traded. That day, people bought and sold ₹538.93 crore of Nippon India's gold ETF, ₹3.68 crore of Aditya Birla Sun Life's and ₹0.13 crore of Choice's.

What does each one cost you every year?

5. The Yearly Cost

Every fund takes a small share of its money, a little each day, to pay its costs. This is called the expense ratio.

  • Aditya Birla Sun Life: 0.47% a year, for 2024-25.
  • Nippon India: 0.80% a year, for 2024-25.
  • Choice: its fund house estimates up to 1% a year, plus some allowed extra costs. Its current cost is on the fund's website.

So how do you compare them before you buy?

6. How to Compare Gold ETFs Before You Buy

  1. Check the extra. During the day, look at the iNAV, an estimate of the NAV. If the price on your app is far above it, you pay extra. Our ETF article shows where to find the iNAV.
  2. Check how much it trades. On your app or the NSE website, look at how much of the ETF was bought and sold that day.
  3. Check the yearly cost. Find the expense ratio in the fund's documents or on its website.

If you already hold a gold ETF: do steps 1 and 3 for the one you hold. We do not tell anyone to buy, keep or sell. An adviser registered with SEBI can look at what you own.

If you are buying your first gold ETF: do all three steps, and compare at least two funds.

If you put in money every month: do step 1 each time you buy, because the extra can change.

Key Takeaways

  • Before you buy, check the price against the iNAV.
  • Some gold ETFs trade very little.
  • The yearly cost differs from fund to fund.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do gold ETFs hold real gold?

All three in this article say they invest in physical gold (see section 1).

Why is a gold ETF's price different from its NAV?

Buyers and sellers decide the price, but the NAV comes from the gold the fund owns (see section 1).

Is a gold ETF better than digital gold?
