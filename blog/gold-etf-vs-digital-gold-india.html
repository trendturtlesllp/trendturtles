SEBI watches gold ETFs, not digital gold. Digital gold complaints rose from 17 in 2020 to 112 in 2025.

Digital gold buyers also pay 3% GST on every purchase. On 3 November, Nippon India's gold ETF unit sold at ₹112.69, almost its real value of ₹112.75.

Complaints: the finance ministry's written reply in the Lok Sabha, 15 December 2025, reported by Upstox and Dhanam Online; the 2025 number is for January to November. SEBI caution, 8 November 2025. Prices: NSE daily price file and AMFI real value list for 3 November 2026.

By Mayank Gola, TrendTurtles  ·  7 min read  ·  Published: November 2026

In 2020, state governments in India received 17 complaints about buying and selling digital gold. In 2025, up to November, they received 112. The finance ministry told Parliament this on 15 December 2025, as Dhanam Online and Upstox reported. Even so, NPCI, which runs UPI, says people bought digital gold through UPI about 12.34 crore times in November 2025 alone.

A gold ETF and digital gold both follow the price of gold. So which is better? This article puts ₹10,000 into each on 3 November 2026.

1. Gold ETF or Digital Gold, Which Is Better? Start With Who Watches Each One

A gold ETF, or exchange traded fund, is a fund. A fund collects money from many people and buys something with it. Nippon India's gold ETF says its aim is to follow the price of gold in India "through physical gold". The fund is sold in small pieces called units. You buy units on the stock market through an app. For this you need a demat account, an account that holds your shares and units.

Digital gold is gold you buy inside an app, such as Paytm or PhonePe. A company keeps the gold for you in a vault. You need no demat account.

SEBI, the body that makes rules for the stock market, watches gold ETFs. On 8 November 2025, SEBI said digital gold products "operate entirely outside the purview of SEBI". In simple words: SEBI does not watch them. SEBI also said that the stock market's ways of protecting buyers do not apply to them. Our article on digital gold explains what nobody checks, and what happened to buyers on three apps.

Now, what does each one cost you on the day you buy?

2. What You Pay When You Buy

Say you put ₹10,000 into digital gold. The app adds 3% GST, the tax on buying gold. So your ₹10,000 buys gold worth about ₹9,709. The other ₹291 is tax.

Now say you put ₹10,000 into Nippon India's gold ETF on 3 November. One unit ended the day at ₹112.69. So ₹10,000 buys 88 units for ₹9,916.72, and ₹83.28 stays with you. Gold ETF units are treated as securities, like shares. The GST department says a sale of securities "is not a supply in GST and hence not taxable". In simple words: there is no GST on gold ETF units. Your broker, the company whose app you use to buy, may charge a fee.

Each unit also has a real value, the value of the gold behind it, called the NAV (net asset value). At that day's NAV, the 88 units were worth ₹9,922.41.

What do you get back when you sell?

3. What You Get Back When You Sell

When you sell digital gold, the app buys it back from you at its sell price. Groww, a share trading app that also sells digital gold, says gold "like any other commodity which is tradable has a buy-sell spread". In simple words: the app buys gold back from you at a lower price than it sells it.

In April 2024, a user wrote about this on the forum of Jupiter, a money app. The app showed the user's invested value, after paying 3% GST, as ₹60,363.28. To sell it, the app offered ₹58,283.78. The user asked: "where is the remaining 2079.50???" That is about 3.4% less than the app showed the gold was worth. If your app offered the same 3.4% less, your ₹9,709 of gold would bring back about ₹9,374. This is one user's number, not a rate any app has published.

A gold ETF unit is sold on the stock market, to another buyer, at that moment's price. On 3 November, Nippon India's gold ETF's price and its NAV were only about 6 paise apart. So if you sold your 88 units at that day's closing price, you would get back ₹9,916.72, before any broker fee.

But a gold ETF does not always sell close to its real value.

4. When a Gold ETF Also Gives Back Less

In January 2026, many people rushed to buy gold ETFs, Upstox reported. When many people want to buy, the price can go above the real value.

On 21 January 2026, Nippon India's gold ETF ended the day at ₹134.97. Its NAV was ₹127.02. So buyers paid about 6% more than the real value. On that day, ₹10,000 bought 74 units for ₹9,987.78. The next day, the price was ₹124.34, just below the NAV of ₹124.57. The NAV fell about 2%. Those 74 units, sold the next day, brought back ₹9,201.16. That is ₹786.62, or about 8%, less than the ₹9,987.78 paid for them.

6. How to Check Before You Buy

  1. For digital gold, see the sell price before you buy. Open the sell page in the app and note the sell price for 1 gram. Compare it with the buy price at the same time. The gap is what you lose if you sell at once.
  2. For a gold ETF, compare the price with its real value. During the day, there is an estimate of the NAV, called the iNAV. If the price on your app is far above it, you pay extra. Our ETF article shows where to find the iNAV and how to work out the extra.
  3. Check the yearly cost. For a gold ETF, find the yearly cost, called the expense ratio, in the fund's documents. For digital gold, read the app's terms for charges to keep your gold.

If you already hold digital gold: do step 1 today, so you know what you would get back. We do not tell anyone to buy, keep or sell. An adviser registered with SEBI can look at what you own.

If you save a small amount every month: some apps let you start digital gold with ₹10, Upstox reports. With a gold ETF, you buy at least one unit, about ₹113 on 3 November.

If you are buying gold for the first time: read section 1, then do steps 1 and 2.

Key Takeaways

  • Digital gold adds 3% GST when you buy. Gold ETF units have no GST.
  • Selling digital gold gives back less than its buy price.
  • A gold ETF's price can rise above its real value.
  • Digital gold needs no demat account, and some apps let you start with ₹10.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a gold ETF backed by real gold?

Yes. Nippon India's gold ETF says it follows the price of gold "through physical gold" (see section 1).

Is digital gold safe?

SEBI says digital gold is outside its watch (see section 1).

Why are gold ETF prices different from the NAV?

Buyers and sellers decide the price on the stock market, but the NAV comes from the gold the fund owns (see sections 2 and 4). Our article on ETF prices explains it in full.

What about sovereign gold bonds?

Sovereign gold bonds, sold by the government, are a third way to own gold. Our article on gold bond tax explains what changed for them on 1 April 2026.
