Home › Blog › Trading App Real or Fake

Fake trading apps on WhatsApp groups show fake profits. Delhi investor says the app showed ₹5.5 crore, and the group asked ₹66 lakh to withdraw it.

Indians lost ₹22,495 crore to cyber fraud in 2025, and investment scams made up 75% of it.

Source: Delhi Police case, reported by The420 on 24 October 2026; loss figures from ThePrint, 21 February 2026.

By Mayank Gola, TrendTurtles  ·  4 min read  ·  Published: November 2026

Is a trading app real or fake? An investor in west Delhi learned the answer only after paying. The investor says it began in a WhatsApp group, where people said they were a company's staff and financial consultants. They suggested an online trading app. The app showed gains, so the investor kept sending money, in many payments, to bank accounts the group gave.

Soon the app showed about ₹5.5 crore as the investor's money. On 5 August 2026, the investor asked to take money out. The investor says the group first asked for ₹66 lakh as "service charges". The investor paid ₹10 lakh to take out part of the money, and the group then asked for ₹23 lakh more. The investor told the police the loss is ₹64.4 lakh, The420 reported. The police have filed a case, and these claims are not yet proved in court.

This article shows how such apps work, and how to check any app before you pay.

1. How These WhatsApp Groups Work

SEBI, the body that controls the share market, warned on 22 October 2026 that "certain unregistered persons are offering stock tips and trading calls to investors through invitation-only WhatsApp groups". In simple words: people with no permission from SEBI run private WhatsApp groups and give share tips. Registered means SEBI has allowed the firm to work.

The group's owners pretend to be market experts, mostly using foreign numbers. Some members post fake profits and praise the "expert", so others trust the group. When a group is closed, its members are asked to join a new one.

Then the group suggests an app, which shows profit. Why can that profit not be taken out?

2. Why the Money on the Screen Is Not Real

SEBI asks investors to deal only with firms registered with SEBI. A broker is a firm that SEBI allows to buy shares for you, and your money goes to your account with that broker. In the Delhi case, the investor says the money went to bank accounts the group gave. SEBI says fake apps make people think their money is in the share market. If the investor is right, the app's money was only a number on the screen.

Is this one unlucky person, or a bigger problem?

3. Not Just One Case

The420's report has a second case. A person in Rohini, Delhi, says they lost about ₹57 lakh on an investment site from social media. So how do you check an app before you pay?

4. How to Check if a Trading App Is Real or Fake

SEBI gives three checks: Check, Validate, Verify.

  1. Check the bank account. Before you pay, check the account number in SEBI's Saathi app, under "SEBI Check".
  2. Validate the UPI ID you pay to. SEBI says to check that the UPI ID has the word "Valid" in it. You can confirm it in SEBI Check too.
  3. Verify the app. On Google Play, the apps of brokers registered with SEBI show a "Verified" label. Look for it before you install. On an iPhone, find the app's company on SEBI's list of registered brokers instead.
  4. Do not pay to take money out. If an app asks for fees first, stop paying.
  5. Report it fast. Call 1930, the national helpline for online money fraud, or file a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in. Keep screenshots and payment receipts.

Key Takeaways

  • Money sent to bank accounts a group gives does not reach the share market.
  • Before paying, check the app, the bank account and the UPI ID with SEBI.
  • If an app wants fees before you take money out, stop and report it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Verified label work on iPhone apps?

No. SEBI started the label on Google Play. On an iPhone, find the app's company on SEBI's list of registered brokers (see section 4).

Is every app shared in a WhatsApp group fake?

Treat it as unsafe. SEBI says do not open an account on, or send money to, any trading app suggested in these groups (see section 4).
