1 in 4 Jewellers Don't Follow India's Gold Verification Rule

By Mayank Gola, TrendTurtles  ·  7 min read  ·  Published: October 2026

Since April 2023, every piece of gold jewellery sold in India is supposed to carry a HUID — a code that lets you verify its purity. Three years on, about 1 in 4 jewellers still don't display it. BIS has had to start raiding shops and filing criminal cases to enforce a rule that was meant to protect you by default.

If the regulator can't guarantee it, the honest answer is: you have to check it yourself, every time, before you hand over money or gold.

1. Is the Jeweller BIS-Registered?

BIS keeps a public list of registered, hallmarking-licensed jewellers. A shop that isn't on it has no official authority to sell hallmarked gold at all. Check before you buy, not after.

2. Does the Gold Have a Verifiable HUID?

HUID is a 6-character code engraved on the piece. Open the free BIS Care app, type it in. It shows you the purity, the registered jeweller, and the date it was tested. If the app says "Record Not Found" — that's the 1-in-4 problem showing up in front of you. Walk away.

3. Will They Show You Documentation Without Hesitation?

Ask for the certification behind a piece. A genuine seller produces it immediately. Reluctance, vague answers, or a sudden change of subject is the clearest red flag there is — it means there's nothing to show.

4. Is the Pricing Broken Down, Line by Line?

Gold value, making charge, and GST should appear as three separate lines, not one "all-inclusive" number. The making charge alone is usually 12-25% of the gold value, set by the shop, not the government — we've broken down the full math and a real court case here: Gold Making Charges in India: What They Really Cost You.

5. Are They Pushing You to Decide Right Now?

"Today only," "last piece," "price going up tomorrow" — these are pressure tactics, not facts about gold. A legitimate jeweller doesn't need to rush you into a decision. If you feel hurried, slow down on purpose.

A jeweller in Chennai ran gold-savings schemes for years, collecting monthly deposits with the promise of gold at the end. When it collapsed, he owed more than 21,000 customers over ₹75 crore in gold they had already paid for.

That's what this problem looks like at its worst — paying in advance, over months, for gold you don't hold yet. It's the riskiest version of the same question this whole piece is about: can you verify, before you commit, that the person on the other side will do what they said?

Key Takeaways

  • About 1 in 4 Indian jewellers still don't comply with the mandatory HUID display rule — regulatory enforcement alone isn't enough, you have to verify yourself.
  • Check five things before you buy, exchange, or save with any jeweller: BIS registration, a verifiable HUID, willingness to show documentation, itemized pricing, and no pressure tactics.
  • "Record Not Found" on the BIS Care app means walk away — a fake hallmark can be stamped on, a fake HUID can't pass the database.
  • Gold-savings schemes carry the highest risk, since you pay before you receive anything — one Chennai jeweller owed 21,000 customers ₹75 crore when his scheme collapsed.
  • Making charges, the one cost that's almost always under-explained, usually run 12-25% of the gold value — the full math is in a separate piece, linked above.
