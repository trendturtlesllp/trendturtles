Home › Blog › Is This Trading App Safe

RBI lists 95 forex platforms and promoters as unauthorised. Users can also face legal action under FEMA.

The list had 34 names in 2022. A Pune office worker took a gold loan to put ₹8 lakh into forex trading. His complaint led to a probe of OctaFX, an app now on the list.

Source: RBI's Alert List, updated on 19 November 2025; Business Standard, 7 September 2022; ThePrint, 19 October 2024.

By Mayank Gola, TrendTurtles  ·  7 min read  ·  Published: November 2026

Harshal Pinjan, an office worker in Pune, got WhatsApp links and videos about forex trading from someone he knew. Forex trading means buying and selling currencies, like dollars, to profit when prices change. He was promised his money would double or even triple. So he took a gold loan and withdrew his LIC investments to put ₹8 lakh into a forex app.

He found he had been cheated, and went to the police in December 2021, ThePrint reported. In 2022, the Enforcement Directorate (ED), the agency that probes money crimes, started its own case against OctaFX. So is the trading app on your phone safe? First, see what these apps sell.

1. What These Apps Sell

Some trading apps sell "binary options". In a binary option, you bet whether a price will be up or down at a set time.

US regulators say a binary option pays "either a pre-determined amount of cash or nothing at all". They add that "the expected return on investment is actually negative". In simple words: over many bets, the buyer loses money.

So how do these apps reach people in India?

2. How These Apps Reach You

RBI, India's central bank, says these platforms advertise on social media, search engines, OTT platforms and gaming apps, and that their agents also contact people directly. ED says OctaFX was promoted through IPL, social media and celebrities.

Some people sell "training" or "advice" about these apps on social media and video sites, or offer "demo trading" with practice money. RBI warns about these too. SEBI, the body that controls the share market, warns about invitation-only WhatsApp groups, and asks people not to open an account on any app suggested there.

And when people pay, where does the money go?

3. Where the Money Goes, and Why Users Lose

ED says OctaFX took money through UPI and bank transfers, into the accounts of fake Indian firms and of individuals. Then it moved the money through many "mule accounts". Mule accounts are bank accounts in other people's names, used to pass money on. Part of the money went abroad as fake payments for software, and part was kept in cryptocurrency.

ED also says early users got small profits "to build trust", while the app used fake price charts to make sure users lost. By ED's count, OctaFX took about ₹1,875 crore from Indians in just nine months, July 2022 to April 2023. ED has frozen assets worth over ₹2,681 crore, so they cannot be sold or moved. The case is still in court, so no court has yet decided whether OctaFX broke the law.

A pharmacist in Chinchwad, Pune, says that in 2025 another "binary trading" app showed him big profits, then blocked his account when he tried to take money out. So is there an official list of such apps?

4. RBI's Alert List, and Four Apps We Checked

RBI keeps an Alert List of apps and websites that are not authorised to offer forex trading. Authorised means RBI has allowed it under FEMA, India's law on foreign currency. The list also names promoters, the people and firms that advertise such apps. Its latest update, on 19 November 2025, has 95 names. RBI also keeps a list of authorised platforms.

Google searches in India for Pocket Option, Olymp Trade and Deriv rose in the 12 months to 31 October 2026. On 16 November 2026, we searched both RBI lists for these three apps and OctaFX.

Deriv is on neither list. So an app missing from the Alert List is not always allowed. What happens to people who trade on an app RBI has not allowed?

5. What the Law Says About Users

RBI says people in India who trade forex on platforms it has not allowed "shall render themselves liable for legal action under the FEMA". In simple words: the user can face action too, not only the app. Paying in rupees does not change this, RBI says.

Under FEMA, the penalty can go "up to thrice the sum involved". In simple words: up to three times the money used in the trade. We found no reported case of a user being fined so far. The cases we found are against the apps, their agents and promoters. So what can you do to stay safe?

6. How to Check if a Trading App Is Safe

  1. Search RBI's Alert List. Open the Alert List and use "Find in page" in your phone's browser menu to search the app's name. If it is there, RBI has warned against it.
  2. For forex, check RBI's list of authorised platforms. Open the list and search the name. If it is not there, RBI has not allowed it.
  3. For shares, check SEBI. SEBI keeps a list of registered brokers, firms allowed to trade shares for you. Open it and search the name of the company behind the app. On Google Play, a registered broker's app shows a "Verified" label.
  4. Do not send money abroad for trading margin. Margin is the money a platform asks you to put up before you trade. RBI's FAQ says LRS, the scheme for sending money out of India, cannot be used for this.
  5. Never pay to take money out. If an app asks for fees before you can withdraw, stop paying.
  6. If you have paid, report it fast. Call 1930, the national helpline for financial fraud, or file a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in. RBI's FAQ also names ED and the state police.

If someone sent you an app link or a "training" offer: do steps 1 to 3 before you install anything.

If you already trade forex on an app: do steps 1 and 2 today, and step 4 before your next payment.

If you have lost money: do step 6 today, and do not pay anyone who promises to get it back for a fee.

Key Takeaways

  • Binary options pay a fixed amount or nothing, and regulators say buyers lose over time.
  • OctaFX, Olymp Trade and Pocket Option are on RBI's Alert List; a name missing from it is not proof of RBI approval.
  • Check RBI's and SEBI's lists before you send money to any trading app.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a legal way to trade currencies in India?

Yes, on India's stock exchanges (NSE, BSE and MSE), or on platforms RBI has authorised. But dollar-rupee and other rupee trades are allowed "only for the purpose of hedging". In simple words: only to protect against a real currency risk, such as a payment due abroad, not to bet.

Can a court make Google refund money lost on a trading app?

One High Court said no. In March 2026, the Madhya Pradesh High Court turned down a man who had lost money on Olymp Trade and wanted Google to refund him. The court said he downloaded the app "with wide open eyes" and invested on his own. In simple words: he chose the app himself, so Google is not to blame.

Are binary options banned anywhere?

Yes. In 2018, the European Union's market regulator banned selling binary options to ordinary investors.

Does SEBI's Verified label cover forex apps?

No. It is for share trading apps of SEBI-registered brokers (see section 6).
