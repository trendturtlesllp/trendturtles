Digital gold is gold you buy in a phone app. You pay a small amount, from as little as ₹1. The app says it buys real gold for you and keeps it in a vault. You never see the gold. You trust the app.

Many people now save this way. In January 2026, people made 21.9 crore digital gold purchases through UPI, worth ₹3,926 crore, according to NPCI data. NPCI runs UPI. In September 2026, digital gold purchases were ₹2,590 crore, 83% more than a year earlier, the World Gold Council said. The two figures are measured in different ways, so they cannot be compared with each other.

Is Digital Gold Safe in India? What the Rules Say

SEBI is the government body that watches the share market. On 8 November 2025 it gave the public a warning about digital gold. It said these products "operate entirely outside the purview of SEBI". In simple words: digital gold is not SEBI's job, and SEBI's protection does not cover you.

The RBI is the body that watches banks. In August 2026, the news agency KNN reported that the RBI watches only the payment system you use to buy. It does not watch the gold itself. The magazine Moneylife put it plainly: digital gold is not under SEBI, not under the RBI, and not under any other financial regulator.

What Nobody Checks

Because no regulator watches digital gold, no government body checks that the gold is really in the vault. Regulators could not inspect the platforms' vaults, Business Standard reported. Your safety depends only on the company behind the app.

Nithin Kamath heads Zerodha, a firm through which people buy shares and gold ETFs. A gold ETF is a gold fund sold on the share market. He wrote: "Most people don't realize that nobody regulates digital gold." If something goes wrong, he said, "there's not much you can do." Keep one thing in mind: Zerodha also runs a gold ETF of its own.

The police can still act. The Karnataka High Court said criminal law applies to digital gold too. But the police come after something goes wrong. Nobody checks before.

What May Change

After SEBI's warning, many buyers took their money out. Withdrawals from digital gold apps nearly tripled, Business Standard reported in November 2025, citing industry sources.

The government is now preparing rules. In August 2026, KNN reported that it plans a formal framework for digital gold by next year, which means 2027. The plan is that every unit sold must be backed by real gold in insured vaults, checked by regular independent audits. This is a plan, not a law. Until it becomes law, nobody watches.

What Happened to Digital Gold Buyers

Here are three real cases, each with a link.

1. Jar: the app's bank accounts were frozen (2026)

Jar is a digital gold app. In 2026, the police in Bengaluru opened a case against it. In March 2026, the Karnataka High Court refused to stop the police case. The judge noted that investors had been saying to each other "there is no gold and no money". In simple words: some investors feared the app had neither the gold nor their money. This is a claim, not proven. No court has found Jar guilty, and Jar says the claim is wrong. The High Court also noted several complaints from the public online.

Jar told the court that it buys real gold every time, and that the gold is kept in a vault by a company called Brink's India. In April 2026, a lower court in Bengaluru noted that the police had confirmed about 1,521 kg of gold kept with Brink's India. But it also noted that, in the case itself, no customer had complained of not getting gold or money.

The police froze Jar's bank accounts while they looked into the case. Frozen means no money can go out. Jar's lawyer told the High Court that, with the accounts frozen, Jar could not pay customers who asked for their money. In August 2026 the High Court ruled that the police may freeze bank accounts during a case. So the freeze can stay while the case goes on, except for some essential payments.

2. Plus Gold: the company stopped work (2025)

Plus Gold is a digital gold app that was shown on the TV show Shark Tank India. The news site Inc42 reported on 31 July 2025 that the company had stopped work, because it could not raise money to keep going. Some users complained that they could not take their money out. The founder said no money was stuck, and that another gold company had taken over the app.

3. Paytm: no gold delivery for about eight months (2025 to 2026)

Some buyers want the real gold one day, as a coin. Several Paytm gold buyers told the news site Moneycontrol that Paytm paused gold delivery in August 2025. The app only said "coming soon". Paytm said it was rebuilding the way it sends gold to buyers. In April 2026, Paytm announced delivery across more than 12,000 pin codes. Paytm said buyers could still sell their digital gold for cash during the pause.

21.9 crore digital gold purchases in one month. No financial regulator watching the apps.

What It Costs You

You lose 5 to 6% on day one. Digital gold costs money even when nothing goes wrong. Business Today lists two costs. The first is GST of 3%, the tax on buying gold, which you pay the moment you buy. The second is the spread, about 2 to 3%. The app sells gold to you at one price and buys it back at a lower price; that gap is the spread. Kamath put it in rupees: "You're instantly looking at a ₹500–₹600 loss on a ₹10,000 investment."

You may not get your money when you need it. You save a little every day, and one day you need the money. The company may have stopped work, as Plus Gold did. Its bank accounts may be frozen, as Jar's were. Even when you can sell, you may wait months for the gold coin you wanted, as Paytm buyers did. The cost is the small problem. Not being sure you can get your own money is the big one.

Digital Gold vs Gold ETF vs Bank Gold Coin

These are three ways to buy gold without buying jewellery. A gold ETF is a fund that holds gold. You buy a small part of it, called a unit, on the share market. For that you need a demat account, which holds your shares and funds.

None of the three is free of trouble. But with a gold ETF, SEBI is watching.

Digital gold fees. The news site Storyboard18 lists three costs: 3% GST, storage, and the app's fees. It says they "often result in differences between the buying and selling prices". In simple words: you buy high and sell low.

The ETF trap. Some days an ETF sells for more than the gold inside it is worth. The money blog BasuNivesh gives one case. On 21 January 2026 a big gold ETF sold at 5.96% more than its gold was worth. The next day its price fell 7.88%. The gold inside it fell only about 1.92%. In simple words: the buyer lost about 6% more than gold did.

The yearly fee of an ETF is small. The website GoldenPi reported fees of 0.59% to 0.69% a year at three big funds in mid-2026. That is about ₹59 to ₹69 a year on ₹10,000.

The bank coin trap. The website Goodreturns reported in October 2024 that RBI rules do not let a bank buy back a gold coin. This is true even for a coin the same bank sold. You pay extra to buy it. Later you sell it to a jeweller, often for less.

What To Do While the Rules Are Unclear

There are seven steps, and each takes a few minutes.

  1. Ask who keeps the gold. Open the terms and conditions page of the app. Look for the name of the vault company. If you cannot find it, do not pay.
  2. See both prices on the same day. Look at the app's buy price and its sell price. The gap, plus 3% GST, is your loss on day one.
  3. Sell a little first. Before you save for months, sell ₹100 of gold. See if the money comes to your bank.
  4. Never pay to take your money out. Fake apps also use the name "digital gold". If an app asks for a "tax" or a fee before you can take money out, stop. In a 2026 police case, a man in Mangaluru says he lost ₹10.55 lakh; bank officials told him it was a fraud.
  5. Want SEBI to be watching? Pick what it watches. SEBI's warning names gold ETFs. It also names electronic gold receipts. These are electronic records that stand for real gold. They are bought and sold on the stock exchange.
  6. Buying an ETF? Check what it is really worth. The fund company's website shows a number called iNAV. It is what one unit of the fund is worth right now. If the market price is more than 1% above it, wait. BasuNivesh says to use a limit order. That means you fix the price you will pay.
  7. Buying a coin from a bank? Ask one thing. "Will you buy it back?" Hear the answer before you pay.

Who needs this most:

  • You save ₹10 or ₹100 a day in an app. Do steps 1 to 3. Small amounts become big savings. SEBI has said it is not watching them.
  • You are buying an ETF for the first time. Do step 6. Do not buy on a day when everyone is rushing to buy.
  • Your family is buying a coin for Dhanteras or as a gift. Do step 7. A bank coin is easy to buy and harder to sell.

Key Takeaways

  • People made 21.9 crore digital gold purchases through UPI in January 2026, worth ₹3,926 crore.
  • SEBI says digital gold is not its job, and the RBI watches only the payment. No regulator checks that the app holds your gold.
  • The government plans rules by 2027, with real gold behind every unit and regular audits. Until then, nobody watches.
  • In real cases, one app's bank accounts were frozen, one company stopped work, and one app paused gold delivery for about eight months.
  • By one estimate, a digital gold buyer loses about 5 to 6% on day one: 3% GST and a spread of about 2 to 3%.
  • Sell a little first, never pay a fee to take money out, and if you want SEBI watching, pick a gold ETF.
