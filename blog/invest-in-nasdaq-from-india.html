3 ways to invest in the US Nasdaq from India. The easiest way, the Nasdaq 100 ETF, now costs 16.6% more than its real value.

On 9 November 2026, one unit of Motilal Oswal Nasdaq 100 ETF cost ₹298.59 on NSE, the stock exchange, but was worth ₹256.11. The gap changes from day to day. The other two ways each have a limit.

Source: NSE's daily price file for 9 November 2026, and the real value list of AMFI, the group of fund companies, for 9 November 2026.

By Mayank Gola, TrendTurtles  ·  8 min read  ·  Published: November 2026

How to invest in Nasdaq from India? There are 3 ways. The Nasdaq 100 is a list of 100 big companies on Nasdaq, a stock market in the US. Funds in India can buy these shares for you.

This article follows Motilal Oswal Nasdaq 100 ETF, whose price was far above its value on 9 November 2026.

This is part of our series on what you really buy in an ETF. Our ETF article explains why price and value differ.

1. The 3 Ways to Invest in Nasdaq from India

Way 1: a fund. You give money to an Indian fund company, and it invests in the US for you. There are two kinds. An index fund buys the Nasdaq 100 shares itself. ICICI Prudential Nasdaq 100 Index Fund is one. A fund of funds buys units of another fund that holds the shares. For example, Motilal Oswal Nasdaq 100 Fund of Fund buys units of Motilal Oswal Nasdaq 100 ETF. Navi and Axis also run Nasdaq 100 funds of funds.

A fund is sold in small pieces called units. You buy units from the fund company, at the real value of one unit that day. This real value is called the NAV. You can put in money once, or a fixed amount every month through a SIP, when the fund allows it. Each fund also charges a small fee every year. Bought without an agent, these four funds charged 0.19% to 0.63% a year in October 2026.

Way 2: an ETF. An ETF, or exchange traded fund, is also a fund. But you buy and sell its units on NSE through your stock app, like a share. You can buy even one unit. Two ETFs on NSE follow Nasdaq lists. Motilal Oswal Nasdaq 100 ETF follows the Nasdaq 100. Motilal Oswal Nasdaq Q50 ETF follows the 50 companies next in line to join the Nasdaq 100. You pay the price on the app. Buyers and sellers decide this price, so it can be different from the NAV.

Way 3: US shares yourself. You open an account with a broker in the US, a company that buys and sells shares for you. Then you send money from your bank. The RBI, India's central bank, allows this under a rule called the Liberalised Remittance Scheme, or LRS. Under LRS, one person can send up to US$250,000 abroad in a financial year, from April to March. With that money, you can buy US shares, or a US ETF such as Invesco QQQ, which follows the Nasdaq 100.

In a fund, you pay the NAV. But why is it often closed to new money?

2. Why Nasdaq Funds Often Take No New Money

Indian funds can invest abroad only up to a fixed limit. SEBI, the body that watches the share market, sets this limit. All Indian fund companies together can invest up to US$7 billion abroad. One fund company can invest up to US$1 billion. When the limit is full, a fund document filed with SEBI says the fund "will not be able to invest in overseas securities". In simple words: it cannot buy more shares abroad.

So the fund stops taking new money. ICICI Prudential Nasdaq 100 Index Fund stopped new one-time investments and new SIPs from 2 March 2026. SIPs that were already running continued. People who held units could still sell them.

Motilal Oswal Nasdaq 100 Fund of Fund has been closed for longer. It has not taken one-time investments since 17 January 2022, or new SIPs since 10 December 2024. Its fund document of May 2026 says it has taken no money even from running SIPs since 5 January 2025.

A fund can open again when the limit has room. So a fund is open only some of the time.

The ETF can be bought on your stock app every day. But its price can be above the NAV. Why?

3. The ETF Is Open, but You Pay Extra

Normally, when many people want to buy an ETF, new units are made, and this keeps the price near the NAV. But Motilal Oswal said in 2022 that this ETF makes new units only while the limit is not full. So when the limit is full and buyers want more units than sellers offer, the price goes above the NAV.

On 9 November, Motilal Oswal Nasdaq 100 ETF cost ₹298.59 for a unit worth ₹256.11. So a buyer paid ₹42.48, or 16.6%, more than the shares behind the unit were worth. For Motilal Oswal Nasdaq 100 ETF, the extra was 18.8% on 1 June, 22.0% on 1 September and 8.2% on 30 October. So the extra you pay depends on the day you buy.

You can also lose this extra. If the fund can make new units again, the price can fall back to the NAV, even if the Nasdaq does not fall. Our article on ETF prices shows how buyers of Motilal Oswal Nasdaq Q50 ETF lost this way in 2026.

So the ETF is open, but you may pay well above the real value. Is there a way to pay only the price of the shares?

4. Buying US Shares Yourself Through LRS

Buy in the US yourself, and you pay the US market price, with no Indian fund limit. But two rules come with it.

First, TCS. TCS means "tax collected at source": your bank keeps part of the money you send. For investing abroad, the bank Standard Chartered says it is 20% of the amount above ₹10 lakh in a financial year. This is not an extra cost. The law counts it as tax already paid. It reduces the income tax you pay.

Second, reporting. Every year, your income tax return must list your shares abroad in a part called Schedule FA. If you miss it, the penalty can be ₹10 lakh.

In 2023, a tax court in Mumbai, the court that hears tax disputes, agreed with the penalty. The person had sent money through LRS into a fund, but did not list it in Schedule FA. A tax website reports the penalty was ₹10 lakh for each of three years.

What should you check before you choose?

6. What to Check Before You Invest

  1. Check if the fund is open. On the fund company's website, open the fund's page and read its latest notice. The notice says if the fund takes new money.
  2. If you buy the ETF, check how much extra you pay. Compare the price on your app with the iNAV, the NAV estimated during the day. Our ETF article shows where to find it.
  3. If you use LRS, count your money for the year. Add up everything you send abroad from April to March. TCS starts above ₹10 lakh. Keep the TCS certificate your bank gives you, and list the shares in Schedule FA when you file.

If you are starting a SIP: do step 1 every month, because a fund can stop new SIPs at short notice.

If you already hold the ETF: do step 2 before you sell or add more.

If you send more than ₹10 lakh a year: do step 3 before you send the money.

Key Takeaways

  • In a fund you pay the NAV, but it often takes no new money.
  • The ETF's price can be far above its value, by a different amount each day.
  • LRS has no fund limit, but has TCS and yearly reporting.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I start a SIP in a Nasdaq fund from India?

Only if the fund is taking new SIPs that month. A fund can stop new SIPs when its limit for investing abroad is full (see section 2).

Is the Nasdaq 100 ETF the same as buying Nasdaq shares?

It holds the same shares, but its price on NSE can be above the value of those shares (see section 3).

Is the 20% TCS on LRS a loss?

No. It counts as tax you have already paid (see section 4).

How is my gain taxed if I buy US shares through LRS?

If you hold them for more than 24 months, the gain is taxed at 12.5%. If you sell earlier, the gain is added to your income and taxed like the rest of it.
