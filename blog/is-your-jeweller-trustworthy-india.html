Only 18% of Gold Buyers Surveyed in India Said Their Jewellery Had a Real Hallmark Code

By Mayank Gola, TrendTurtles  ·  6 min read  ·  Published: October 2026

A hallmark is the government's stamp on a piece of gold. It says the gold is as pure as the seller claims. A real one carries a six-character code. In April 2025, a LocalCircles survey asked people who had bought gold jewellery in the past year how long the code on it was. It got 29,853 answers:

  • 18% said six characters, which is correct.
  • 27% named a wrong length: three, four or five.
  • 28% said their jewellery had no code at all.
  • 27% could not say.

This was an online survey of the platform's own members, so read it as a strong signal, not a census. The signal is clear enough. Only about one buyer in five could point to the code that makes a hallmark checkable.

When you cannot check the gold yourself, you do the only thing left. You trust the jeweller. That is the gap where buyers get hurt, and it takes one minute to close.

What a Real Gold Hallmark Looks Like

The stamp comes from the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS), the government body that sets quality standards. Since July 2021, BIS says a hallmark has three marks:

  • The BIS logo.
  • The purity. Karat is the measure of how pure gold is, and 24 karat is pure gold. So 22K916 means 22 karat, which is 91.6% gold.
  • A six-character code of letters and numbers, called the HUID. Every piece has its own.

If one of the three is missing, it is not a complete hallmark. The marks are tiny. Ask the jeweller to show you all three under a magnifying glass.

Two things to know. Hallmarking is compulsory in 380 districts as of March 2026, not in every district. And LocalCircles notes that some styles, such as Kundan, Polki and Jadau, do not need one. For any other gold piece, no hallmark means you have no way to check its purity. Choose one that has it.

The One-Minute Hallmark Check

You do not have to take the stamp on trust either. BIS has a free app called BIS Care. Open it, choose "Verify HUID", and type the six-character code from the piece. BIS says this confirms the hallmark is authentic, and you can do it before you buy. If the app cannot find the code, or shows a different purity from the one stamped on the piece, do not buy it.

The same BIS page gives you a right most buyers have never heard of. If hallmarked jewellery turns out to be less pure than its mark, the buyer is entitled to twice the difference in value, plus the testing charges. BIS also says the bill must carry the hallmark details if you ever need to complain, so ask for one that does.

Only 18% of buyers surveyed said their jewellery carried the six-character code. Without that code, a hallmark cannot be checked.

Why Not Knowing Turns Into Trust

The same survey asked buyers a second question. Was the gold jewellery you bought in the last 12 months hallmarked? Of 16,784 people, 65% said yes. 11% said no. 24% could not say.

So about one buyer in three could not confirm it. The 2024 survey found the same thing: 65% said yes, and the rest said no or could not say. A buyer who cannot read the stamp has to rely on the person selling it.

Where That Trust Breaks

When you buy

A hallmark covers purity. It does not cover price. The making charge, which is the labour cost of turning gold into jewellery, is set by each shop. In a case reported in 2018, a customer was promised 3–5% and billed 23.5%, and a consumer court in Bangalore ordered the jeweller to compensate her. The full story and the maths are in Gold Making Charges in India: What They Really Cost You.

When you exchange old gold

Here the jeweller tests your gold and then tells you what it is worth. The value should be simple: weight, times purity, times the day's rate. After that, the lender IIFL Finance puts the usual refining or melting charge at 1 to 3%. The test should happen in front of you. A hallmark on your old piece helps, because the purity is already certified.

When you join a savings scheme

In a gold savings scheme you pay every month and collect gold at the end. You pay first. That is the risk. A hallmark cannot help you, because there is no gold in your hand yet.

In November 2017, The News Minute reported that the Chennai jeweller Nathella Sampathu Chetty had admitted to police that it owed ₹75 crore to more than 21,000 people who had paid into its schemes. One customer told its reporters he had paid in about ₹1.3 lakh over three years. The company had already closed seven shops.

A legal explainer on LiveLaw notes that jewellers keep these schemes within 11 to 12 months, because of company deposit rules. A scheme that asks you to keep paying for longer than a year is a reason to ask questions.

What To Do Before You Pay

Six steps. The first two take a minute.

  1. Look for the three marks. BIS logo, purity, six-character code.
  2. Type the code into BIS Care. Do it in the shop, before you pay.
  3. Ask for a full bill. Purity, weight, the code, making charge and GST (the tax), each on its own line.
  4. Exchanging old gold? Watch the test. Get the weight, the purity and every deduction in writing.
  5. Joining a scheme? Check its length. It should end within a year. Keep a receipt for every payment.
  6. Feeling rushed? Wait a day. A fair price will still be there tomorrow.

Who needs this most:

  • A family buying for a wedding. It is the biggest gold bill you will pay, and old gold is often exchanged in the same visit. Do steps 1 to 4 on every piece.
  • Someone saving a little each month. Step 5 is yours. Your risk is the shop, not the gold.
  • A first-time or gift buyer. Steps 1 and 2 are enough to make sure the piece is what the tag says.

Key Takeaways

  • In a 2025 survey of gold buyers in India, only 18% said their jewellery carried the six-character hallmark code.
  • A real hallmark has three marks: the BIS logo, the purity, and a six-character code.
  • The free BIS Care app checks that code in about a minute, before you pay.
  • If hallmarked gold is less pure than its mark, BIS rules entitle you to twice the difference, so keep the bill.
  • A hallmark protects purity only. On exchange, watch the test. In a savings scheme, check the shop and the scheme's length.
