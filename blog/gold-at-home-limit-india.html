Family Gold - "500 gms. per married lady, 250 gms. per unmarried lady and 100 gms. per male member of the family need not be seized"

No law sets a limit on gold at home. There is an instruction the tax department gave its officers in May 1994, a government statement from 2016, and tribunals that read them differently. Until the rule is clear, your papers decide what you keep.

CBDT (income tax department) Instruction No. 1916, issued on 11 May 1994. Source: Taxmann, a tax publisher.

By Mayank Gola, TrendTurtles  ·  8 min read  ·  Published: October 2026

What is written: an instruction the tax department gave its officers in May 1994, ref. 1916. In a raid, they do not have to take 500 grams of jewellery per married woman, 250 grams per unmarried woman and 100 grams per man. The officer may leave more, if the family's customs explain it.

What is not written: any limit on how much gold you may own. In 2016 the government said there is none, if you can explain where the gold came from.

Where it gets unclear: what counts as "explained". Some officers treat 500 grams as a ceiling. Tribunals have said it is not. One family won with no bills. Another lost part of its case with only wedding photos. And many people still read the numbers in that instruction as a legal limit. As the tax publisher Taxmann notes:

"Many people believe that CBDT Instruction No. 1916 … lays down limits to how much jewellery an individual can possess."

What that costs: gold the tax office finds and you cannot explain can cost about ₹99 of every ₹100.

What to do while it stays unclear: keep papers for every piece. The steps are below.

How Much Gold Can You Keep at Home in India? No Fixed Limit

"There is no limit on holding of gold jewellery or ornaments by anybody provided it is acquired from explained sources of income including inheritance."

Government press release, 1 December 2016. Source: Taxmann; date from CAalley.

In simple words: you can keep as much gold as you like. But you must be able to say where it came from. Gold from your parents counts as a good answer.

So the real question is not "how much gold?". It is "can you explain it?". Gold you cannot show the source of is called "unexplained".

One more thing. The May 1994 instruction speaks of gold jewellery and ornaments. Gold bars are a different matter, as one family found out in the Delhi High Court.

What Families Went Through

Here are three real cases. A tribunal is a tax court where you can appeal. All three cases come from tribunal or court rulings, as reported. Each has a link, so you can read it yourself.

1. A mother's 263 grams of gold

In September 2021, tax officers searched a man's home in Pune. His mother, 67, had 263.83 grams of gold. CAalley reports that the tax office counted ₹11.32 lakh of the family's gold as his hidden income. He would have paid tax on it.

He appealed. An appeal officer cancelled the ₹11.32 lakh. The tax officer had even missed the 500 grams allowed for his wife. In October 2025, the tax tribunal in Mumbai agreed. It said this was jewellery families usually keep, within reasonable limits.

The tribunal also said this about the instruction: jewellery up to its limits "cannot, without further evidence, be regarded as unexplained investment." In simple words: gold within the limits is taken as normal, unless the tax office has other evidence.

2. A family of nine with no bills

Tax officers searched a family home in Ghaziabad. Indian Jeweller reports they found 3,877.5 grams of jewellery, worth ₹1.46 crore. The officer accepted 2,250 grams for nine family members. The officer called the other 1,627.5 grams unexplained. The tax office split it among three family members. Each was taxed on ₹20.45 lakh.

The family had no purchase bills. But the tax tribunal in Delhi looked at the family's income over the years. It accepted that the gold came from inheritance, weddings and gifts. It cancelled the extra tax. This was reported in October 2026.

In simple words: the numbers in the instruction are not a ceiling. A family can explain more, if its history backs it up.

3. Gold bars in a locker

Officers found 1,242 grams of gold in a woman's bank locker. She said it was her jewellery and her daughter-in-law's. Taxscan reports that 426 grams of it were 24-carat gold bars. That is pure gold, not jewellery.

The Delhi High Court kept the tax on the bars. It said the family jewellery reason did not explain them. This was reported in July 2026.

In simple words: "it is family gold" works for old ornaments. It does not work for bars without their own proof.

The Impact: Unexplained Gold Can Cost Almost All of Its Value

A new income tax law began in 2026. It changed the tax on unexplained income, and that includes gold. Outlook Money explains it:

  • If you report it yourself: the tax is 30%. With extra charges, about 39%.
  • If the tax office finds it first: a penalty is added. Outlook Money gives an example from tax experts: on ₹100 found this way, about ₹99 can go in tax and penalty.
  • If you accept it and do not appeal: the penalty can be cut, to about 75% in all.

Gold found in a raid is found by the tax office. So here is an example with round numbers. It is not anyone's real case. Say a raid finds ₹1 lakh of gold that you cannot explain. You could lose about ₹99,000 of it.

And the gold is yours. It may have come from your grandmother. The tax is not for having gold. It is for not being able to show where it came from.

Selling Inherited Gold: How the Tax Works

Getting gold from your parents is not taxed. Outlook Money quotes a tax expert: "When gold is received as inheritance or gift, there is no capital gain." Selling it is a different matter.

When you sell, you pay tax on your gain. Your gain is the selling price, minus what the gold cost. Three rules decide the tax:

  1. Your cost is what your parent paid. A tax expert in the same Outlook Money article says so.
  2. Add your years to your parent's years. More than 24 months in all? The tax is 12.5% of the gain. Angel One, a broking firm, sets this out.
  3. Very old gold has a shortcut. If it was bought before 1 April 2001, you can use its value on that date as the cost. Angel One says this too.

Angel One adds a warning. If you cannot show your parent could have owned that much gold, it may be treated as unexplained.

What To Do About Your Family Gold

The rule may never be fully clear. These steps protect you either way. You can do most of them in one evening.

  1. List every piece. Write its weight and take a photo. Write whose it is: mother, wife, daughter, son. The Pune family's case was decided person by person.
  2. Write where each piece came from. Wedding, gift, inheritance or purchase, with the year. The Ghaziabad family won without bills, because its income history backed its story.
  3. Keep every bill you have. For new gold, ask for a GST bill. That is a proper shop bill with the tax number on it. Outlook Money's expert calls this "essential".
  4. Keep proof for gold bars and coins. Keep the bill and the bank record for each one. The locker case was about bars. Coins are pure gold too, so treat them the same way.
  5. Keep the papers for inherited gold. Keep the will, or the legal papers that show you are the heir. Note roughly when your parent got each piece.
  6. Before you sell inherited gold, work out the tax. Use your parent's cost and the total years held. If it is from before 2001, use its value on 1 April 2001.

Who needs this most:

  • Your family keeps gold from many generations. Do steps 1, 2 and 5. Your story is your proof.
  • You are about to sell your parents' gold. Do steps 5 and 6 first.
  • You buy gold bars or coins as savings. Do step 4 for every one.

Key Takeaways

  • The 500 g, 250 g and 100 g rule in the May 1994 instruction is not a limit. It says how much jewellery tax officers do not have to take in a search.
  • The government said in 2016 there is no limit on gold you can explain, including gold you inherited.
  • Tribunals have cancelled this tax. Sometimes the gold was above these amounts. The family could explain it.
  • Gold bars need their own proof. "Family jewellery" did not cover them in a 2026 Delhi High Court case.
  • Gold you cannot explain costs about 39% if you report it yourself, and about 99% if the tax office finds it.
  • When you sell inherited gold, your parent's cost and years count. After 24 months in all, the tax is 12.5% of the gain.
